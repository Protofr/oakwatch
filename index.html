Symptom Check

Answer 5 quick questions about your oak. It takes about a minute.

Step 1: Checklist

Question 1 of 5 What kind of oak is it?

Live oaks have small, oval, leathery leaves that stay green most of the year. Red oaks (like Texas red oak and Shumard oak) have bigger leaves with pointed lobes.

Question 2 of 5 Do you see leaves with yellow or brown VEINS?

Look closely at a few leaves. The veins (the thin lines in the leaf) turn yellow, then brown, while the rest of the leaf stays green. On live oaks, this is the clearest sign of oak wilt.

Question 3 of 5 Are leaves browning from the tips or edges?

The leaf turns pale or brown starting at the tip or outer edge, moving inward. This is common on red oaks with oak wilt. (Drought can cause it too.)

Question 4 of 5 Is the tree suddenly dropping lots of leaves?

Heavy leaf drop over a few weeks, especially in spring or summer when oaks usually keep their leaves. Fallen leaves may still be partly green.

Question 5 of 5 Have nearby oaks died recently?

Oak wilt often kills oaks in a group that slowly grows outward, because it spreads through connected roots. Several dead or dying oaks close together is a warning sign.

Keep watching your oaks

  • Check a few leaves every couple of weeks in spring and early summer, when oak wilt is easiest to spot.
  • Look for veins turning yellow or brown on live oak leaves.
  • Watch for oaks in your neighborhood dying in a group.
  • Paint every pruning cut and wound right away. Any paint works.
  • If anything changes, take this check again.

This is not a diagnosis. Only a lab test or qualified arborist can confirm oak wilt.

Safe to Prune?

Why does this matter?

Oak wilt is a fungus that kills oak trees, especially live oaks and red oaks. It spreads in two main ways:

  • By beetles: Tiny sap beetles carry the fungus from sick trees. They are drawn to the smell of fresh cuts and wounds on healthy oaks. They are most active in spring.
  • Underground through roots: Oaks growing near each other often have connected roots. Once one tree is infected, the fungus can travel through the roots to neighboring trees, even across property lines.

That's why one careless pruning cut can put a whole block of trees at risk.
